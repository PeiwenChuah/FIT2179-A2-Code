Healthcare In Malaysia

Exploring workforce, infrastructure, disease burden, and financing across Malaysia's health system.

Overview Infrastructure Workforce Disease Burden Finance

Section 1

National Healthcare Overview

Malaysia's population has grown substantially since 2000, yet birth rates have fallen steadily while death rates remain low and stable — reflecting an ageing, urbanising society. This section compares two contrasting states to reveal how access to care differs within the country.

Health Indicator Trends (2000–2023)

Birth & death rates per 1,000 population — measured annually.

Key insight Birth rates fell from 23 to below 16 per 1,000 — a 30 % decline in two decades, signalling demographic transition. Death rates remain flat near 5, showing improved survival outcomes.

Healthcare Indicators: Sarawak vs Selangor

Normalised scores (0 = lowest, 1 = highest) across six healthcare dimensions.

Key insight Selangor leads in Doctors and Pharmacists, reflecting its urban advantage. Sarawak scores higher on Hospital Beds per capita — likely driven by rural facility investment to serve dispersed communities.

Section 2

Healthcare Infrastructure

Hospital bed distribution reveals stark regional disparities. Urban districts in Selangor and Perak hold the most beds, while many rural districts in Sabah and Sarawak remain significantly under-resourced.

Key insight Kinta (Ipoh) and Johor Bahru districts account for the largest single-district bed counts. Comparing years shows bed capacity grew faster in Selangor than in East Malaysia states.

Section 3

Healthcare Workforce

Malaysia has significantly expanded its healthcare workforce over the past three decades, though distribution between states and professions remains uneven.

Staff Count by Type (2014–2022)

Absolute staff numbers across four healthcare professions. Nurses form the largest share throughout.

Key insight Total healthcare staff grew by ~50% between 2014 and 2022. Pharmacist numbers grew fastest, nearly doubling over the period.

Professionals per 10,000 Population (1990–2023)

Rate-adjusted view removes population growth bias and shows true service availability trends.

Key insight Despite a larger workforce, nurses per 10,000 showed high variation — likely due to reporting methodology changes. Doctor rates have grown steadily, from ~4 to ~18 per 10,000.

Doctors per 10,000 Population by State

Geographic distribution of registered doctors in the most recent year available. Darker shade = higher doctor density.

▲ W.P. Kuala Lumpur and W.P. Putrajaya are the small territories on the west coast of Peninsular Malaysia — labelled directly on the map. Hover over a state for exact values.

Key insight W.P. Kuala Lumpur has more than 60 doctors per 10,000 population — four times the national average. Sabah and Kelantan remain the most under-served states.

Section 4

Disease Burden

Understanding which conditions drive the most hospital admissions — and how public versus private sectors absorb patient volume — is essential for resource planning.

Top 10 Hospitalisation Causes (2022–2024)

Rank-over-time bump chart. Each line traces one disease category. Rank 1 = most admissions.

Key insight Respiratory conditions rose from Rank 2 in 2022 to Rank 1 by 2024 — overtaking Pregnancy & Childbirth. Infectious & parasitic diseases climbed three places, possibly reflecting post-pandemic surveillance.

Inpatient & Outpatient Visits by Sector (2024)

MOH Public facilities dominate both inpatient and outpatient volumes.

Key insight MOH Public handles over 65 M outpatient visits — nearly 10× the private sector volume. Private facilities handle a disproportionately higher share of inpatient stays relative to outpatient.

Section 5

Healthcare Finance

Malaysia spent RM 89.8 billion on healthcare in the latest National Health Accounts period. The Sankey diagram below traces how funds flow from financing sources through intermediaries to providers and final functions.

Flow of Health Funds

Total Health Expenditure: RM 89,827 M · Hover over a flow to see source and destination values.

Key insight Despite public financing (RM 45.6 B) slightly exceeding private (RM 44.2 B), out-of-pocket payments (RM 34.8 B) form the single largest private source — a significant financial burden on households.
