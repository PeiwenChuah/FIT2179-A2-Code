Section 1

National Healthcare Overview

Birth and Death Rates, 2000–2023


Delayed marriages and declining urban fertility actively drive Malaysia toward an ageing population, mirroring societal shifts in Japan and South Korea. Furthermore, falling birth rates and the 2020 pandemic mortality spike rapidly accelerate the closing gap between births and deaths.

State Healthcare Comparison, 2024


The western region holds a clear advantage in healthcare capacity, with greater access to professionals, support staff, and facilities. This stronger infrastructure underscores an uneven distribution of resources nationwide, leaving the east comparatively less equipped to meet population needs.

Section 3

Healthcare Workforce

Total Staff Count, 2014–2022

The steady rise in healthcare staff from 2014 to 2022 reflects a deliberate push to strengthen Malaysia’s health system. Nurses drive most of the growth, while parallel increases in doctors, pharmacists, and dentists signal a more balanced workforce. This expansion points to a strategic effort to broaden coverage and reduce accessibility gaps nationwide.

Staff Rate per 10,000 Population, 1990–2023

The trend in healthcare professional density from 1990 to 2023 is upward, reflecting improvements in workforce capacity. Doctors and pharmacists show growth, while nurses remain the largest group despite fluctuations. This upward trajectory signals progress in strengthening Malaysia’s health system and expanding access to care.

Section 4

Disease Burden

Top 10 Admission Causes, 2022–2024

Hospital admissions from 2022 to 2024 reveal deeper pressures on the health system. Respiratory diseases remain dominant, reflecting vulnerability to outbreaks and air quality. Digestive cases are rising, pointing to lifestyle-driven demands that strain resources. Perinatal admissions decline, suggesting gains in maternal and child health. Overall, Malaysia’s system must balance entrenched respiratory burdens with emerging non-communicable challenges.

Inpatient and Outpatient Visits by Sector, 2024

Malaysia’s healthcare demand in 2024 is largely carried by the MOH public sector, reflecting affordability but also creating strain on government capacity. Private hospitals contribute mainly to inpatient care for wealthier groups, while their outpatient role remains limited due to cost barriers. Non‑MOH public facilities are minimal, reinforcing the system’s heavy reliance on MOH as the central national safety net for all citizens and ensuring universal healthcare access.

Section 5

Healthcare Finance

Flow of Health Funds — RM 89,827 Million

Financing sources → Aggregators → Providers → Health functions



Malaysia’s health financing in 2024 is almost evenly split between public and private sources, showing that households and government contribute at similar levels. Yet the allocation reveals a strong bias toward hospital-based curative care, while preventive services receive only a fraction of funding. This imbalance suggests that while resources are substantial, the system is oriented toward treatment rather than prevention, which may limit long-term sustainability and keep the population vulnerable to avoidable health burdens.
