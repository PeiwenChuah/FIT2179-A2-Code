Section 1

National Healthcare Overview

Malaysia's health story over two decades is one of demographic change, fewer births, an ageing population, and persistent inequalities between states. COVID-19 briefly reversed mortality gains in 2020–21.

Birth and Death Rates (2000–2023)

Key Insight

Birth rate fell from 23 to below 16,

a 30% drop signalling demographic ageing.

Death rate, flat near 5, spiked sharply in 2021

as COVID-19 hit vulnerable populations hardest.

Section 2

Healthcare Infrastructure

Hospital beds reveal where resources are concentrated. Urban districts in Peninsular Malaysia dominate, while East Malaysian districts remain significantly under-resourced relative to population need.

Click a state block to zoom into its districts · Click the chart title to zoom back out

Key Insight

Kinta (Perak) and Johor Bahru hold the most beds

of any single district in the country.

Sabah and Sarawak lag despite serving vast areas,

distance to the nearest hospital is a real barrier.

Section 3

Healthcare Workforce

Malaysia has more than doubled its healthcare workforce since 2000, yet where those professionals practise matters as much as how many there are. Kuala Lumpur alone has four times the national average of doctors.

Total Staff Count (2014–2022)

Absolute headcount across four professions. Nurses form the largest share throughout.

Key Insight

Total healthcare staff grew ~50% in eight years.

Pharmacists nearly doubled, the fastest growth

of any profession, driven by expanded retail

and hospital pharmacy services.

Rates per 10,000 Population (1990–2023)

Population-adjusted view removes growth bias and shows true service availability trends.

Key Insight

Doctor rate grew steadily from ~4 to ~18 per 10,000.

Pharmacist growth surged post-2010

as community pharmacy roles expanded.

Nurse numbers show high variance,

likely from changing reporting definitions.
